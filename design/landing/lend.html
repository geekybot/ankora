Lend dollars, and choose how much of the loss you take.

Deposit USDC on NEAR, and it goes straight into the pool. Cross-chain deposits through 1Click are on the roadmap. Every loan is backed by NEAR, so pooling can't remove crash risk. Your choice of Fixed 6.5% or First-loss decides who takes a loss first.

Fixed 6.5%

Senior tranche

Loses only after First-loss
  • Interest is paid before First-loss earns anything. Fixed 6.5% money is lost only after the borrowers' equity and every First-loss deposit are gone.
  • 3-month US Treasury bills paid 4.21% on 2 Nov 2026. The extra 2.3 points pay you for a NEAR crash tail, a new contract and slower exits.
  • We tested the 2021–22 crash with a rate model fitted only on later prices. Fixed 6.5% was paid in full in every start-week when collateral sold within 2 days, and in 94.3% of them when sales took 5 days at a 10% discount.
  • Withdrawals are paid from cash the pool isn't lending. When there isn't enough, you wait for borrowers to repay: terms run up to 30 days, and a loan in trouble takes up to 6 more days to close. v1 has no withdrawal queue, so when cash is short, whoever withdraws first is paid first.

First-loss

Junior tranche

Variable · can lose it all
  • You take losses before Fixed 6.5% lenders. In a bad enough crash you lose your whole deposit.
  • You keep what the pool earns after Fixed 6.5% and Ankora's fee. Replaying 2021–22 on these plans gave 18.1–21.0% a year, in-sample. Fitted only on 2023–26 prices and tested on 2021–22, the same pricing rule gave 11.3%, with losses in 4.8% of weeks.
  • Locked for 36 days from your latest First-loss deposit; adding more restarts the lock on all of it. After that, you can't withdraw if it would leave First-loss below 15% of the loans outstanding, and new loans start only while First-loss covers 15% of loans outstanding plus the new loan.
  • Built for holders already long NEAR. Losses here arrive in the same crashes that hit the rest of their NEAR.
Deposit as First-loss

Who takes a loss first.

1. The borrower's equity the vault is worth more than the debt
2. First-loss at least 15% of loans outstanding
3. Fixed 6.5% only after First-loss is gone

How lenders can lose money.

  • Fixed 6.5% and First-loss can both lose money in a fast NEAR crash, and First-loss can lose all of it.
  • Every loan is backed by NEAR, so they all fall together. Since 2020 NEAR has fallen 52% in 3 days and 70% in 35 days.
  • Collateral sells slowly. Unstaking takes about 28 hours, and the liquid-staking pools on Rhea's DEX are $0.5–4.4M deep.
  • Getting your money out depends on borrowers repaying, and v1 pays withdrawals first come, first served.
  • If an auction gets no bid, the team sells the NEAR by hand and books the USDC back to lenders. You rely on them to do it promptly and honestly.
  • On testnet a relay run by the team copies the price to the pool, and the contract can't check it.
  • Ankora is new code and hasn't been audited. In v1 the pool contract can still be upgraded by its deployer.
What's the difference between Fixed 6.5% and First-loss?

Both lend your USDC to people borrowing against NEAR. Fixed 6.5% earns a set 6.5% a year and is paid first. It loses money only if a crash uses up every First-loss deposit. First-loss takes losses first and keeps what the pool earns after paying Fixed 6.5%. In our out-of-sample test on the 2021–22 crash that came to 11.3% a year, and a bad enough crash wipes it out. In tranche terms, Fixed 6.5% is the senior tranche and First-loss is the junior.
