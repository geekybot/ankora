Borrow dollars against your NEAR. A 3am dip can't sell it.

Ankora checks the price when your loan starts and when it renews, and at no other time. If NEAR has fallen too far by renewal, you get five days to pay some back before anything is auctioned.

Rhea liquidates a loan the moment NEAR's price touches its liquidation line, at any hour, and the liquidator buys the collateral at a discount.

We replayed NEAR's daily prices from October 2020 to November 2026 against Rhea's line, at about 66.5% LTV. Each simulated borrower started at 30%, 40% or 50% and added nothing for a year. Of the borrowers who were liquidated, 60–68% were caught on a day when NEAR closed back above their line.

Ankora doesn't look at the price in the middle of a term, so a dip like that has nothing to set off.

      How a loan works.

      Bring NEAR

      Your loan gets its own vault, which stakes the NEAR with a validator you pick from the lockup whitelist. NEAR that's already staked has to be unstaked first, which takes about 28 hours.

      Borrow

      Pick a plan and receive USDC on NEAR. The first term's interest comes off the top.

      Price checked

      Keep earning

      Your staking rewards build up in the vault while the loan runs.

      Renew or repay

      At the end of each term the loan renews by itself if it still fits a plan. Repay, and the vault and its stake are yours.

      LTV 50% or lower

      The loan renews into the plan that fits. The next term's interest is added to your debt.

      LTV above 50%

      72 hours of notice. Pay back part of the debt in USDC to bring LTV to 50% or lower, and the loan renews at the next check. If NEAR recovers in that time, it renews without a payment.

      After the grace period

      A 24-hour Dutch auction of the vault, starting at its full value and falling to half. The lenders are repaid, and anything above your debt goes to you. You can still repay in full until a bid lands.

      If the auction raises less than the debt

      Or nobody bids: the lenders take the loss, starting with First-loss deposits. You keep the dollars you borrowed and owe nothing more.

      No late fee

      If the loan renews after its expiry date, you pay interest for the days since expiry at the new plan's rate.

      If you switch renewal off

      You can switch renewal off whenever you like. If you then don't repay at expiry, the 48-hour grace period starts straight away, followed by the auction.

      v1 sends no email or message when a notice starts. The notice shows in the app and on-chain, so check in around your renewal date.

      In our replay of 2021–22, 53–81% of borrowers who added nothing were asked to pay down at least once in a year. On Rhea, 55–85% of the same borrowers were liquidated. On Ankora each of those requests came with five days to respond before any auction, and any auction surplus went back to the borrower.

      Native stakers keep their validator.

      About 91% of staked NEAR, roughly $1.88B, is native stake with a validator its owner picked. Staking pools can't transfer stake between accounts, so moving it means unstaking once. Rhea doesn't accept native stake. Ankora's vault re-stakes with your validator, as long as it's on NEAR Foundation's lockup whitelist (lockup-whitelist.near on mainnet; testnet uses whitelist.f863973.m0).

      The move costs about 28 hours without rewards, roughly 0.017% of your stake (about 1,700 NEAR on 10M). When you repay, ownership of the vault passes to you with the stake still staked. Stake inside lockup contracts and veNEAR in House of Stake can't be used.

      Lend USDC.

      Two ways to lend. Fixed 6.5% earns a set 6.5% a year, is paid first, and loses money only after First-loss is used up. First-loss takes losses first and keeps what the pool earns after that. Fitted on 2023–26 prices and tested on the 2021–22 crash, it made 11.3% a year, and a bad enough crash wipes it out.

      You want more than 50% LTV

      Rhea lends up to about 66.5%.

      You want a loan with no end date

      Rhea's loans are open-ended.

      You hold a liquid staking token

      Rhea takes it as collateral directly. Ankora v1 takes plain NEAR only.

      You borrow far below the line

      Rhea's main borrowers sit near 19% LTV and rarely get liquidated.

      You want the lowest rate in an ordinary year

      Rhea's 12-month median borrow rate is 7.8%, and at that rate Ankora costs 2 to 3.5 points more. On 10 Nov 2026 Rhea charged 13.99%, after a spike.

      How you can lose money here.

      Borrowers

      • If your LTV is above 50% at a renewal and you don't pay down within 72 hours plus the 48-hour grace period, your vault is auctioned. The auction price can fall to half the vault's value before someone bids.
      • v1 doesn't email or message you when a notice starts. If you don't check the app, the five days can pass without you seeing them.
      • Repaying early doesn't refund the current term's interest.
      • After NEAR's issuance cut, staking covers less of your interest.

      Lenders

      • Fixed 6.5% and First-loss can both lose money in a fast NEAR crash, and First-loss can lose all of it.
      • Collateral sells slowly. Unstaking takes about 28 hours, and the liquid-staking pools on Rhea's DEX are $0.5–4.4M deep. In our tests, slow and discounted sales are what put Fixed 6.5% money at risk.
      • Every loan is backed by the same asset, so they all fall together. Since 2020 NEAR has fallen 52% in 3 days and 70% in 35 days.
      • Getting your money out depends on borrowers repaying, and v1 pays withdrawals first come, first served.
      • If an auction gets no bid, the vault goes to the pool. In v1 the team then sells the NEAR by hand and books the USDC back to lenders, so lenders rely on the team to do that promptly and honestly.

      Everyone

      • Ankora is new code and hasn't been audited.
      • In v1 the pool contract can still be upgraded by its deployer, and the team switches to the stressed terms by hand. The loan vaults can't be upgraded.
      • The price is NEAR/USD from Pyth Pro, read through Rhea's oracle. On testnet a relay run by the team copies it to the pool and works out the one-hour average. The contract can't check the relay's numbers, so you rely on the team for the price used at every start and renewal.
      • If the price is stale, uncertain or missing, loans don't start or renew. A renewal held up this way charges the extra days at the new plan's rate.
      • Rates come from a model fitted on six years of data. When we fitted it on the calm years alone, it underpriced the 2021–22 crash.
      • We haven't tested whether enough lenders, First-loss lenders or auction bidders will show up.

      Questions.

      Do I have to unstake?

      If your NEAR is natively staked, yes, once. It takes about 28 hours and has ranged from 21 to 35. Then you bring plain NEAR and the vault stakes it with your validator. Getting your vault back after you repay needs no unstaking.

      Can I use LiNEAR, stNEAR or rNEAR?

      Not in v1, which takes plain NEAR only. Liquid staking tokens are on the roadmap.

      Does Ankora use an oracle?

      Yes. It reads NEAR's price when a loan starts and when it renews, at no other time, and uses the lower of the spot price and its one-hour average. The price is Pyth Pro's NEAR/USD, the same feed Rhea's lending reads through its aggregate oracle. On testnet a relay run by the team copies that price to the pool and computes the average, so you're trusting the team to copy it faithfully. A loan won't start if the price is more than 90 seconds old or the feed is unsure of it, and your NEAR goes back before anything is staked. A bad price can stop a loan from starting or renewing. It can't take your collateral: only an auction can, and only after the term ends and the notice and grace periods have passed.

      Will I be told when a notice starts?

      In v1, only in the app and on-chain. Check in around your renewal date.

      Can I lose my NEAR?

      Yes, if your LTV is above 50% at a renewal and you don't bring it down within 72 hours plus the 48-hour grace period. The vault then goes to a 24-hour auction whose price starts at the vault's value and falls to half. Anything above your debt comes back to you. You can never owe more than the vault is worth.

      What does it cost?

      A fixed rate for each term. You pay the first term's interest upfront, and each later term's interest is added to the debt. If a loan renews late, out of notice or grace, the extra days are charged at the new plan's rate. There's no late fee. Ankora keeps 5% of the interest. Each vault also holds back 1.5 NEAR from what you send, and about 1.2 NEAR of that pays for the vault account's storage.

      Can I repay early?

      Yes, at any time, even during an auction until a bid lands. The current term's interest isn't refunded.

      Which validators can I use?

      Any staking pool on the whitelist the pool was deployed with: lockup-whitelist.near, the list NEAR Foundation keeps for lockup accounts, on mainnet, and whitelist.f863973.m0 on testnet.

      How is the rate set?

      In v1, from a fixed table. The table comes from a formula that prices each loan as a put option on its collateral, using a jump-diffusion model of NEAR fitted to every day since October 2020. It then adds a charge for First-loss capital and assumes 90% of the pool is lent out. A live version of the formula is on the roadmap. A quote is fixed when you borrow, and a term already running is never repriced.

      What's the difference between Fixed 6.5% and First-loss?

      Both lend your USDC to people borrowing against NEAR. Fixed 6.5% earns a set 6.5% a year and is paid first. It loses money only if a crash uses up every First-loss deposit. First-loss takes losses first and keeps what the pool earns after paying Fixed 6.5%. In our out-of-sample test on the 2021–22 crash that came to 11.3% a year, and a bad enough crash wipes it out. In tranche terms, Fixed 6.5% is the senior tranche and First-loss is the junior.

      What is the replay, and how far can I trust it?

      It runs NEAR's past daily prices through Ankora's rules and Rhea's, side by side. It isn't a forecast, and it simplifies: one exchange's daily prices, one fixed Rhea rate, and auctions shown as a range.
