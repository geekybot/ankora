FAQ

What if NEAR drops during my term?

Nothing happens. The pool doesn't read the price until the term ends, so a dip in the middle of a term can't sell your NEAR.

What happens at renewal?

If your LTV is 50% or lower, the loan renews and the next term's interest is added to the debt. If it's higher, you get 72 hours' notice to pay some back, then 48 hours' grace, then a 24-hour auction that starts at your vault's full value and falls to half. You can repay in full until a bid lands, and there's no late fee. With renewal switched off, the loan goes straight to the 48-hour grace period at term end.

Do I keep my validator?

Yes, if it's on the lockup whitelist: lockup-whitelist.near on mainnet, whitelist.f863973.m0 on testnet. Your vault stakes with it. NEAR that's already staked has to be unstaked first, which takes about 28 hours. Each vault holds back 1.5 NEAR, about 1.2 NEAR of which pays for its storage.

Where does the price come from?

Pyth Pro's NEAR/USD, the feed Rhea reads through its aggregate oracle. The pool uses the lower of the spot price and its one-hour average, refuses prices over 90 seconds old or with confidence wider than 1%, and pauses new loans while spot is more than 3% from the average. On testnet a relay run by the team copies the price to the pool and the team's keeper works out the average; the contract can't check either.

What can I lose as a lender?

First-loss takes losses first and can lose everything; it's locked for 36 days from your latest deposit. Fixed is paid first and loses money only once every First-loss deposit is gone. Withdrawals come from cash the pool isn't lending. If an auction gets no bid, the vault goes to the pool owner, who sells the NEAR and books the USDC back to the pool, so lenders rely on the team to do that.

Is it audited?

No. Ankora is new code and hasn't been audited. The pool contract can be upgraded by its deployer key; the loan vaults can't.

Risks

  • New code, not audited.
  • Testnet price is relayed by the team.
  • The pool contract is upgradable.
  • Unsold auctions go to the owner to sell.
  • First-loss lenders can lose everything.
