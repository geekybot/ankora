Ankora docs

Borrow dollars against your NEAR. A 3am dip can't sell it.

Ankora lends USDC against NEAR for fixed 7 to 30-day terms. The price is read only when a term starts or renews, so a dip in between can't sell your collateral: in a backtest of NEAR's 2020–26 prices, 60–68% of liquidations on a typical liquidation line came on a day NEAR closed back above it. Your NEAR stays staked with your own validator the whole time. Lenders deposit from NEAR or from five other chains through NEAR Intents and pick a layer: Fixed is paid first, First-loss earns the rest and can lose everything. Live on NEAR mainnet, capped while unaudited; free on testnet.

Judging in 2 minutes
  1. Watch the demo (1:50).
  2. Read the thesis: why the protocol makes sense, with historical simulations.
  3. Check the live claims: node scripts/verify.mjs (Node 22, no keys). Rerun the numbers: python research/sim.py. A WARN on the oracle line means Rhea's price feed is momentarily stale, not that a claim failed.
  4. Try it free on testnet. Lending needs a little testnet NEAR for gas, plus test USDC from the app's faucet. Borrowing needs about 5.3 testnet NEAR. Faucets dry? Open an issue and we'll send testnet NEAR.
  5. Mainnet proof, no wallet needed: the pool, three NEAR Intents deposits from Polygon and Solana (1, 2, 3) and loan #0. A mainnet borrow waits until the price is under 90 s old; this proof doesn't need a fresh price.
For borrowersPick a plan: 30%, 40% or 50% of your NEAR's value, for 30, 14 or 7 days. Interest is paid upfront. Your NEAR is staked in a vault made for your loan.
For lendersDeposit USDC from NEAR or from Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Polygon or Solana through NEAR Intents. Choose Fixed (paid first) or First-loss (takes losses first).

Plans and the vault

  1. You send NEAR. At least 5 NEAR: 3.5 or more to stake, plus 1.5 that pays for your vault account's storage.
  2. The pool reads the price in the same transaction. If it is missing, stale or moving too fast, the loan doesn't start and your NEAR comes back.
  3. A vault account is created for your loan and stakes the NEAR with the validator you chose. Any validator on NEAR Foundation's lockup whitelist works.
  4. You receive USDC minus the first term's interest. Staking rewards accrue in the vault and stay yours.
  5. Repay any time. The vault is handed to you; unstake and withdraw like any staking account (about 28 hours). The current term's interest isn't refunded.

Native stakers unstake once (about 28 hours) to move their NEAR in. Staking pools have no transfer method.

Renewal, notice and auction

At the end of each term the pool reads the price once.

Loan-to-value at the checkWhat happens
50% or lessThe loan renews. The next term's interest is added to the debt. You can switch renewal off.
Above 50%72 hours' notice to pay down, then 48 hours' grace. No late fee.
Still above after graceA 24-hour Dutch auction of the staked vault, starting at 100% of its value and falling to 50%. The buyer gets the vault. Anything above the debt goes back to the borrower.
No buyerThe vault goes to the owner to unstake and sell; the proceeds are booked to the pool. Losses hit First-loss first, then Fixed.
The auction is a liquidation. What changes is when and how: one price check per term instead of every block, 120 hours to act, no liquidator bonus, and the surplus returned. In a fast crash a borrower at 50% can still end in auction.

Fixed and First-loss

FixedFirst-loss
Earns6.5% a year on the share of the pool that is lent outEverything the pool earns above Fixed's share, after Ankora's 5% of interest
LossesOnly after every First-loss deposit is goneTakes losses first and can lose everything
WithdrawAny time, from idle cashLocked 36 days after your latest First-loss deposit (one full term plus notice, grace and auction)
  • Cover rule: a new loan needs First-loss capital of at least 15% of all loans. A First-loss withdrawal can't take cover below that.
  • Why Fixed earns on lent cash only: if Fixed earned 6.5% on idle cash, First-loss would pay it from its own deposit and lose money until the pool was about 60% lent. The pool keeps an accrual clock that runs at utilisation, so First-loss is never charged for idle cash.
  • Interest vests into share prices over each term, not on receipt, so depositing just before a payment doesn't capture interest others earned.

From another chain (NEAR Intents)

A lender on Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Polygon or Solana deposits without a NEAR bridge or a NEAR wallet balance.

  1. The app asks NEAR Intents 1Click for a quote that delivers USDC to the pool with the message {"depositfor":{"tranche":"senior","account":"you.near"}}.
  2. You send USDC to the one-time deposit address it returns, on your chain.
  3. 1Click swaps into native USDC on NEAR and calls ft_transfer_call on the pool. The pool credits the shares to you.near, not to the relayer.
  4. If anything fails, 1Click refunds to the address you gave on your chain.

Out to another chain

A withdrawal or a loan payout lands as USDC in your NEAR wallet. Send to another chain (under Your account, on mainnet) moves it to Base, Ethereum, Arbitrum, Polygon or Solana in one wallet approval: it registers a one-time NEAR Intents address with USDC (0.00125 NEAR) and transfers to it, then shows the transfer until it arrives. Refunds come back to your NEAR wallet.

NEAR Intents has no testnet, so on testnet the app shows live mainnet quotes and executes nothing.

What isn't cross-chain

You sign in with a NEAR wallet; Ankora has no EVM or Solana wallet sign-in. Borrowing needs NEAR, because the collateral is NEAR staked with a validator.

Prices

  • Mainnet: Rhea's aggregate-oracle.near, which posts signed Pyth Pro prices about every 40 seconds. Pyth's own NEAR feed on NEAR stopped updating on 25 August 2026. The aggregate has no EMA, so the mainnet pool reads spot only.
  • Testnet: a relay the team runs copies the mainnet spot and a 1-hour EMA. The pool uses the lower of the two and refuses new loans when they differ by more than 3%.
  • Age: a price older than 90 seconds is refused. Rhea's oracle is updated by signed reports that Rhea's signers push irregularly (we have seen gaps over 13 minutes), so a mainnet borrow or renewal can wait for the next update. Deposits and withdrawals never need a price.
  • What the price can and can't do: it can refuse a new loan or a renewal. It can never take collateral during a term. Only an auction does that, and only after notice and grace.

Mainnet caps

The mainnet build (cargo near build --features mainnet) is the same code with four differences:

  • Deposits are refused past 200 USDC in the pool, cash plus loans.
  • Loans are refused past 100 USDC in total.
  • The minimum deposit is 0.5 USDC, so a first cross-chain test can be tiny (1 USDC sent through 1Click arrives as about 0.995).
  • The price is spot from aggregate-oracle.near.

The caps are constants. No method raises them; raising them means a new deployment anyone can see.

Research and results

The numbers come from models run on NEAR's daily history, October 2020 to November 2026. Pass criteria were written into each script before it ran, and failures are reported. Rounds tested earlier parameter sets; each row says which.

How the plans were priced

A loan is a default-free bond minus a put on the collateral (Merton, 1974). NEAR moves in jumps, about 8 a year with a 22.5% standard deviation, so the put is priced with Merton's jump-diffusion: of the models tested, it alone never priced below NEAR's actual losses from 2020 to 2026. It rejected anything above 50% LTV: those loans priced at 15–54% a year. The live menu (30% for 30 days, 40% for 14, 50% for 7) is what survived.

Known weak spots

Read these before lending.
  • The default path is 6 days long (72 h notice, 48 h grace, 24 h auction). Round 10 found that a 10% sale discount and a 5-day delay leave Fixed paid in full in only 94.3% of 2021–22 start-weeks. A borrower who lets a loan run to auction in a crash can cost lenders.
  • Out of sample the pricing failed once (S6). Volatility inputs must cover the whole cycle, not recent months.
  • The contract is unaudited and upgradable by one key.

Not tested

  • Whether lenders show up, and at what size, and who buys First-loss (about $150k for every $1M of loans).
  • Whether auctions attract bidders, or whether bidders can collude.
  • How much native stake sits with holders who would use this rather than a liquid staking token.
  • What Fixed lenders do while Rhea's supply rate stays above 6.5%.

Verify it yourself

One command checks every claim on this page against the chain, NEAR Intents and this site. It is read-only and needs only Node 22:

git clone https://github.com/geekybot/ankora && cd ankora
node scripts/verify.mjs

It reads the mainnet pool's code hash, its reproducible-build record, the caps from get_limits, its keys, its USDC registration and the oracle's age; the testnet pool's state; a live 1Click route into the pool; and the hosted pages. To rebuild the contracts and run the tests:

cd contracts && ./build.sh
cargo test -p ankora-core                      # 43 tests
cargo test -p ankora-pool --lib                # 31 tests
cargo test -p ankora-pool --lib --features mainnet   # 5 tests: the caps
cargo test -p ankora-pool --test flow          # 4 flows on a local NEAR sandbox

Run the whole product locally with a time machine (crash the price, skip to a deadline, watch an auction):

cd client && npm install && npm run local
cd app && npm install && npm run dev

Testnet pool state, read-only: near view geekybot-ankora.testnet get_ledger, get_loans, get_auction_history.
